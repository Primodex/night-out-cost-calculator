The Cost of a Night Out Drinking in the 50 Largest U.S. Metros

We estimated the same night out in the 50 largest U.S. metro areas, using bar menu prices, local price levels, local taxes and rideshare rates. Every metro uses one modeled estimate, so all 50 are compared the same way. For reference, 16 metros also have a measured total from 8 or more bars, 18 more have 3 or more sampled bars (12 with 5 or more, 6 with 3 to 4), 12 have 1 or 2, and 4 have none.

  • 3 drinks (a beer, a cocktail and a glass of wine)
  • 1 appetizer
  • Two 5-mile rideshare trips
  • Local taxes
  • 20% tip on the bar bill
Cost of a standard night out in the 50 largest U.S. metros. Column headers are buttons that sort the table.

  • These are estimates for a standard night out, built from sampled menu prices. They are not what residents actually spend.
  • Every metro uses the same modeled estimate for its night out cost and hours of work. The measured total, for comparison, is shown only for the 16 metros with 8 or more bars priced.
  • Price groups are broad bands from the modeled estimate, for all 50 metros: Highest cost, Next highest, Higher cost and All other metros. Order inside a group means nothing.
  • Modeled estimate: a typical menu basket from the metros where we measured menu prices, scaled by each metro’s local price level, plus local taxes, a 20% tip and an estimated rideshare cost. It is not a measured price.
  • For the 34 metros without measured prices, we show the middle price of each item from the bars we sampled (sampled bars, not for ranking). They are not used for any group or rank. Fresno, Hartford, Milwaukee and Riverside have no sampled bars yet.
  • * Atlanta’s local drink tax is not included, because the city does not publish its rate. Atlanta’s total may be slightly low.
  • Nashville’s beer price comes from only 4 bars.
  • Cost of living is the overall price level of each metro area (U.S. average = 100), not only the price of a night out. It comes from the Bureau of Economic Analysis (2024).
  • Median rent is the monthly median gross rent for the metro area, from the Census Bureau.
  • Hours of work uses the local median hourly wage for all jobs, before tax. The median wage column shows that wage for every metro.
  • Rideshare is estimated from published rate cards, for two one-way rides of 5 miles and 15 minutes each, with no surge pricing. Where a metro has no rate card, a national average is used. Rideshare is not taxed or tipped in these estimates.
